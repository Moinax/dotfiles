# Unslop

Edit English or French text to remove AI patterns and add human voice.

## Process

1. Scan for the patterns below.
2. Rewrite. Preserve meaning, match intended tone and language.
3. Add soul (see next section).
4. Self-audit: "What makes this obviously AI generated?" In French:
   "Qu'est-ce qui rend ce texte manifestement généré par une IA ?" Fix
   remaining tells.

Do not invent facts, sources, quotes, measurements, opinions, or personal
experience. Soul must come from the source material and the intended speaker.

## Adding soul

Removing patterns is half the job. Sterile, voiceless writing is just as
obvious.

- **Have opinions.** React to facts instead of neutrally listing pros and cons.
  In French, do the same instead of flattening the text into a neutral summary.
- **Vary rhythm.** Short sentences. Then longer ones that take their time. Mix
  it up.
- **Acknowledge complexity.** "Impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats
  "impressive." In French: "Impressionnant, mais aussi un peu inquiétant" beats
  "impressionnant."
- **Use "I" when it fits.** First person isn't unprofessional. Use "je" the
  same way in French.
- **Let some mess in.** Perfect structure looks machine-made.
- **Be specific.** Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling
  about agents churning away at 3am." In French, not "c'est préoccupant" but
  "il y a quelque chose d'inquiétant à voir des agents tourner à 3 h du matin."

## Patterns to detect and fix

### Content

1. **Puffery.** "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape",
   "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted". Cut puffery,
   state what happened. French: "moment charnière", "témoigne de", "paysage en
   constante évolution", "ouvre la voie à", "laisse une empreinte",
   "profondément ancré".
2. **Name-dropping.** Listing media outlets without context. Pick one, say what
   was said. French: do the same with lists of publications, institutions, or
   public figures.
3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...",
   "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Delete or expand with real
   sources. French: "soulignant...", "garantissant...", "reflétant...",
   "mettant en valeur...", "favorisant...".
4. **Promotional language.** "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking",
   "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", "must-visit". Use neutral
   descriptions. French: "niché", "dynamique", "époustouflant",
   "révolutionnaire", "renommé", "exceptionnel", "incontournable", "à couper le
   souffle".
5. **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest",
   "Some critics argue". Name the source or delete. French: "Les experts
   estiment", "Des études suggèrent", "Certains critiques avancent".
6. **Formulaic challenges.** "Despite challenges... continues to thrive."
   Replace with specific facts. French: "Malgré les défis... continue de
   prospérer."

### Language

7. **AI vocabulary.** Additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance,
   fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), pivotal,
   showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore, vibrant. Replace with
   plain words. French: de plus, en outre, crucial, essentiel, explorer en
   profondeur, pérenne, favoriser, obtenir, synergie, interconnexion, complexe
   as praise, paysage (abstract), charnière, mettre en valeur, fresque
   (abstract), témoignage, souligner, dynamique.
8. **Fancy ways to say "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features".
   Just say "is" or "has". French: "constitue", "représente", "s'impose comme",
   "fait figure de", "se veut", "bénéficie de". Prefer "est" or "a".
9. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly instead. French: "Pas
   seulement X, mais aussi Y", "Non seulement X, mais Y", "Bien plus qu'un X".
10. **Rule of three.** Forcing ideas into groups of three. Use the natural
    number. Same rule in French.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Protagonist, main character, central figure, hero all
    in one paragraph. Pick one, repeat it. French: protagoniste, personnage
    principal, figure centrale, héros. Pick one.
12. **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale.
    List topics directly. French: avoid "de X à Y" and "du X au Y" when they do
    not describe a real range.

### Style

13. **Em dash overuse.** Avoid em dashes entirely. Use periods or commas only
    (no parentheses, no en dashes, no hyphen-as-dash substitutes). Em dashes
    are an AI tell, and reaching for parentheses instead just trades one tell
    for another. If a thought needs separation, end the sentence or use a
    comma. Apply the same rule in French.
14. **Colon overuse.** Colons are fine before a list or example. Not as
    mid-sentence connectors. "If you're coming from traditional automation:
    instead of registering event handlers, you describe conditions" adds
    nothing with the colon. Rewrite to let the point stand on its own without
    comparison framing. "Describing when the scheduler should fire works best
    as plain English." Same meaning, no crutch punctuation. In French, keep
    colons for actual lists and examples.
15. **Boldface overuse.** Don't bold every proper noun or acronym. Same rule in
    French.
16. **Inline-header lists.** The tell is a bold label and colon that restates
    the line: "**Performance:** Performance improved...". Convert those to
    prose. A bold lead-in that ends in a period, names the item, and is followed
    by genuinely new detail ("**Schema in TypeScript.** Tables live in one
    file.") is fine, not a tell. French: avoid labels such as
    "**Performance :** Les performances..." when they merely repeat the line.
17. **Title case headings.** Use sentence case. French headings also use
    sentence case.
18. **Decorative emojis.** Remove from headings and bullets.
19. **Curly quotes.** Replace with straight quotes. Apply this in French too,
    unless exact quoted material must keep its original punctuation.

### Communication artifacts

20. **Chatbot phrases.** "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Of
    course!", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!" Remove. French: "J'espère
    que cela vous aidera !", "N'hésitez pas si...", "Bien sûr !",
    "Certainement !", "J'ai trouvé le coupable !".
21. **Cutoff disclaimers.** "While specific details are limited..." Find
    sources or remove. French: "Bien que les détails précis soient limités...",
    "Faute d'informations supplémentaires...".
22. **Sycophantic tone.** "Great question! You're absolutely right!" Respond
    directly. French: "Excellente question ! Vous avez tout à fait raison !".

### Filler

23. **Filler phrases.** "In order to" becomes "To". "Due to the fact that"
    becomes "Because". "It is important to note that" gets deleted. French:
    "Afin de" often becomes "Pour". "Du fait que" becomes "Parce que".
    "Il est important de noter que" gets deleted.
24. **Excessive hedging.** "could potentially possibly be argued that it might"
    becomes "may". French: "on pourrait éventuellement peut-être considérer
    qu'il serait possible" becomes "peut-être" or a single precise hedge.
25. **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." State specific plans or
    facts. French: "L'avenir s'annonce prometteur."

### Jargon

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** Substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage,
    nexus, primitive (as noun), harness (as metaphor), surface (as in "API
    surface"), bedrock, scaffolding (as metaphor), modality, paradigm,
    gold-plating, ratchet (as metaphor), evacuate (for moving code), endgame,
    north star, flywheel. These read as technical but usually have a plainer
    concrete word. "Substrate" becomes "base". "Wedge in" becomes "add".
    "Vector" becomes "way" or "method". "Gold-plating" becomes "more than the
    job needs". "Ratchet" becomes the mechanism's real name or "a limit that
    only tightens". "Evacuate" becomes "move out". "Endgame" becomes "the last
    phase". Pick the concrete word. French: watch for borrowed terms and
    abstract metaphors such as "substrat", "vecteur", "nexus", "primitive",
    "surface d'API", "socle", "échafaudage", "modalité", "paradigme",
    "surqualité", "cliquet", "évacuer le code", "fin de partie", "étoile
    polaire", "roue d'inertie". Replace them with the actual base, method,
    interface, limit, move, final phase, goal, or feedback loop.

### Plain speech

27. **Say what it does, not how it feels.** "the database stays close at hand",
    "SQL you can read", "types that follow your schema" name a feeling. The fix
    names the mechanism or a number: "`.toSQL()` returns the exact string sent
    to the database", "a column rename fails the build". Ask what the sentence
    tells the reader to do or know, then write that. If you can't restate it as
    a concrete instruction, fact, or number, cut it. One more check: if the
    sentence could appear unchanged in another project's docs, it says nothing
    about this one. Cut it. French: "la base de données reste à portée de main",
    "du SQL facile à lire", "des types qui suivent votre schéma" describe a
    feeling. Prefer "`.toSQL()` renvoie la chaîne exacte envoyée à la base de
    données" or "renommer une colonne fait échouer la compilation".
28. **Shorten or split dense sentences.** If the reader has to backtrack to
    parse a sentence, break it in two or drop clauses. One idea per sentence.
    Same rule in French.
29. **Active voice.** Prefer it. Catch "is/are/was/were + past participle" and
    name the actor: "queries are validated" becomes "the compiler validates
    queries", "the file is parsed by the loader" becomes "the loader parses the
    file". Passive is fine only when the actor is unknown or genuinely doesn't
    matter. French: "les requêtes sont validées" becomes "le compilateur valide
    les requêtes", "le fichier est analysé par le chargeur" becomes "le chargeur
    analyse le fichier".
30. **Cut adverbs, or use a stronger verb.** "runs quickly" becomes "is fast" or
    the number. "significantly improves" becomes the measured delta. An adverb
    propping up a weak verb means the verb is wrong. French: "s'exécute
    rapidement" becomes "est rapide" or the measured duration. "Améliore
    considérablement" becomes the measured change.
